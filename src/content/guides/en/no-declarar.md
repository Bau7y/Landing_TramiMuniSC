## The obligation

Every property owner must declare its value to the municipality where it is located **at least every five years** (Ley N.° 7509, art. 16). In San Carlos it is filed at the Property Tax Section with the official form.

## What the municipality can do

If the declaration is not filed, or is filed late, article 17 allows the municipality to:

- **Value the property on its own**, that is, carry out a valuation without your declaration.
- **Charge a fine** equal to the difference in tax left unpaid.

An up-to-date declaration is also a requirement for the single-property exemption, so not declaring can cost you that benefit too.

## How to catch up

1. Check your property's status: whether it has a current declaration and whether any quarters are unpaid.
2. Fill in the declaration form with the real data of the land and buildings.
3. File it with your ID at the municipality.

Declaring before the municipality carries out a valuation lets you describe the property as it actually is.

## If you were already notified of a valuation

If you disagree with the value, you can file a **motion for reconsideration** (recurso de revocatoria) with the office that made the valuation within **15 business days** of the notice and, if it is rejected, **appeal to the Municipal Council** within the same period (Ley 7509, art. 19). After that, the valuation becomes final.
